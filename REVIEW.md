| Finding | Resolution and evidence |

Other ratios mixed machines, solver configurations, proof-checking costs, or differently defined cases. | Removed the comparison table, the prototype timing and ablation claims, and projected runtimes for larger boards. The one-diagonal-deficit presentation left the signed offset implicit under interchange of the two diagonal families. | Sections 5.4–5.5 derive the conditions from the color identities and distinguish which family has one fewer occupied line. ## Mathematical checks

- **Sections 2–3.** Checked nonempty frames, margin capacities, the eight actions on lines and colors, geometry of side intersections, all corner multiplicities, and the dependence of the side formulas on the relation between $w$ and $h$.
- **Section 4.** Re-derived the intersecting intervals in the margin argument, the injection into occupied interior rows, the Hall capacity inequality, both nonsquare parity exclusions, and the odd square row/column-deficit exclusion.
- **Section 5.** Checked the five deficit categories and all parity subcases against the branch generator. Re-derived the fixed color counts, side offsets and zero-waste cases, the exceptional monochromatic frame, and the shifted linear and quadratic moments.
- **Section 6.** Checked line occupancy equivalences, dominance clauses, zero-deficit cardinalities, non-needy and double-coverage terms, unary counter overflow, offset count equality, modular residues and lexicographic comparisons. In the reduced grid, checked which shifted diagonals have fewer than two border hits. The coverage theorem explicitly extends a placement to a Boolean assignment.
- **Small boards.** Exhaustive bit-mask enumeration found 12, 4, and 0 dominating $n/2$-sets for $n=4,6,8$. The prover agreed with and without within-case lex constraints. On $n=8$ both runs checked every refutation.
- **Upper bounds.** All 12 stored witnesses passed direct checks, including the five independent witnesses. The eight coordinate lists printed in the manuscript agree with the original lists and JSON data.

The current logs record a successful DRAT check for every leaf from complete end-to-end runs of the pinned binary (`queen_prover` with CaDiCaL 3.1.0, `drat-trim`, `--jobs 10 --config auto --certify`). Rebuilding the DIMACS files and comparing all 74,155 digests confirms exact correspondence with the pinned encoder. The raw proofs were temporary and removed after verification.

The controlled 99-leaf benchmark completed three runs per mode. All 297 produced proof traces passed. Its median wall times were 0.33 s without certification and 3.35 s with certification. The median sum of the individual checker times was 2.88 s. The raw measurements and sampling rule are in `results/benchmark-overhead-n26.json` and `bench/verification_overhead.py`.

The full $n=26$ pruning benchmark used the same solver and checker settings and ten workers on one Apple M4. Toggling algebraic pruning reduced the formula count from 7,668 to 2,773 and median complete-command wall time from 58.49 s to 24.39 s, a 2.40× speedup. All six certified runs completed with every proof checked. [Individual runs](results/benchmark-pruning-n26.json).

## Source checks

- **Previous queen bounds.** The even-board two-value range follows from rounding the classical lower bound and the constructions in the original 2001 paper. The journal record gives DOI 10.37236/1573. Cockayne reports Spencer’s lower bound in §2.3 of the author’s report. The independent Raghavan–Venkatesan article is *Information Processing Letters* 25(5), 281–284. Its publisher record was accessible, and its lower-bound attribution was checked against Cockayne, Östergård–Weakley and the frame papers. [Östergård–Weakley](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v8i1r29), [Cockayne](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X9090344H), [Cockayne’s report](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/d4fcc303-a3d6-486a-8550-f0710279cb9e/download), [Raghavan–Venkatesan](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0020019087902018).

The archived *Hint and Conquer* PDF gives the needed ordinary values at 32, 34, and 38. Davies’s Theorem 1.3 and Table 2 give the ordinary values at 40 and 44. The cited OEIS attachment records Đukić’s $n=48$ computation and gives a witness. [Weakley](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v29i2p50), [Neuhaus](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/2916/), [*Hint and Conquer*](https://github.com/TahaRostami/Gamma/blob/hint-and-conquer/doc.pdf), [Davies](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00830v1), [Đukić](https://oeis.org/A075458/a075458_1.txt).
- **Encoding and verification.** Publisher records confirm the authors, titles, volumes, page ranges and DOI links for totalizers, the sequential counter, the CaDiCaL tool paper, and DRAT-trim. The CaDiCaL bibliographic citation is for its 2.0 paper. The reproduction uses the separately pinned 3.1.0 code. [Bailleux–Boufkhad](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-45193-8_8), [Sinz](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11564751_73), [CaDiCaL](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-65627-9_7), [DRAT-trim](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-09284-3_31).

## Remaining limits

All three target boards ($n=50,52,54$) now have complete DRAT-certified runs under the pinned build (`CaDiCaL` 3.1.0 commit `e4d9bd93`, `drat-trim` commit `2e3b2dc0`, `--jobs 10 --config auto`), though tool commit pins, solver configuration, and worker count are fixed by the build and invocation rather than stored inside the JSONL summary line. The controlled pruning benchmark compares two modes of the present prover. The mathematical coverage proof has been audited via AI-assisted review and checked on small boards, and remains a written argument without independent human verification of the proofs or encoder.
